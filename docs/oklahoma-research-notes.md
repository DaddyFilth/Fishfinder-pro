# Oklahoma fishing research notes

## Authoritative sources reviewed

1. Oklahoma Department of Wildlife Conservation, [Sportfish ID](https://www.wildlifedepartment.com/fishing/resources/species). The official sportfish list includes largemouth, smallmouth, spotted, striped, striped hybrid, and white bass; blue, channel, and flathead catfish; black and white crappie; freshwater drum; alligator gar; paddlefish; sauger; saugeye; bluegill, green, and redear sunfish; brown and rainbow trout; and walleye.
2. Oklahoma Department of Wildlife Conservation, [General Fishing Regulations](https://www.wildlifedepartment.com/fishing/regs/general-fishing-regulations). It defines Oklahoma game fish as black bass, black and white crappie, rainbow and brown trout, sauger, saugeye, walleye, white/hybrid/striped bass, and blue/channel catfish. It also states anglers need permission to enter private land; public map records must therefore represent public or explicitly managed access.
3. Oklahoma Department of Wildlife Conservation, [Statewide Daily & Size Limits](https://www.wildlifedepartment.com/fishing/regs/statewide-daily-size-limits). It confirms harvest-regulated sportfish including bass, channel/blue/flathead catfish, crappie, striped bass and hybrids, white bass, trout, walleye/sauger/saugeye, paddlefish, and alligator gar. It also lists black buffalo, bullhead species, freshwater drum, and other nongame or special-concern fish that can occur in Oklahoma waters.
4. Oklahoma Department of Wildlife Conservation, [Fishing Access for Persons with Disabilities](https://www.wildlifedepartment.com/fishing/wheretofish/access). It names public access areas including Adair Park, Arbuckle, Bell Cow Lake, Bixhoma Lake, Boomer Lake, Canton, Claremore Lake, and Crosslin Park, with additional pages available through pagination.
5. Oklahoma Department of Wildlife Conservation, [Updated Atlas of Public Lands and Fishing Lakes](https://wildlifedepartment.com/outdoor-news/updated-atlas-public-lands-and-fishing-lakes-now-available). It describes Department-managed public fishing areas and WMAs, maps with roads/parking/campsites, and more than 1.4 million acres of Department-managed public lands.
6. City of Oklahoma City, [Fishing](https://www.okc.gov/Community-Recreation/Recreation-Activities/Fishing). It lists Oklahoma City public waters including Lake Hefner, Lake Overholser, Lake Stanley Draper, Oklahoma River, Stinchcomb Wildlife Refuge, Crystal Lake, Dolese Youth Park Pond, Edwards Park Fishing Lake, Kids' Lake, Kitchen Lake, Route 66 Park Ponds, South Lakes Park Ponds, and Zoo Lake. It also notes required state/city permits and stocked species including walleye, hybrid striped bass, channel catfish, and hybrid sunfish.
7. Oklahoma Department of Wildlife Conservation, [Fish Identification](https://wildlifedepartment.com/outdoorok/ooj/chapter-5-fish-identification). It says Oklahoma has 176 fish species, with many restricted to parts of the state, and identifies additional Oklahoma species such as rock bass, bowfin, blue sucker, and other stream fish.

## Implementation scope

The map will use a curated statewide public-access dataset, clearly labeled as public/managed access rather than claiming every private or informal bank location. It will include major lakes, reservoirs, rivers, Department PFAs/WMAs, trout areas, and named municipal waters with coordinates. Each record will carry an access/source note and a public-access flag so the UI can distinguish public access from general water bodies.

The catalog will become Oklahoma-only and freshwater-focused. It will include commonly encountered sportfish plus notable Oklahoma species users requested such as buffalo and bullhead, while retaining scientific/encounter metadata where appropriate. Species that are not documented or plausible in Oklahoma waters will be removed from the visible Oklahoma catalog.

## User-provided poster reference

The user-provided “Common Fish of Oklahoma” poster distinguishes a practical Oklahoma encounter list from a strictly game-fish list. Visible named entries include largemouth bass, spotted bass, smallmouth bass, green sunfish, bluegill, white bass, white crappie, black crappie, redear sunfish, saugeye, striped bass, striped bass hybrid, channel catfish, blue catfish, flathead catfish, walleye, spotted gar, longnose gar, American paddlefish, sauger, rainbow trout, freshwater drum, smallmouth buffalo, and common carp. The product should present these as Oklahoma encounters, with a separate `Game fish` or harvest-status classification rather than filtering the catalog to only game fish.

The poster is a user-supplied local reference and should be treated as a practical visual guide, while official ODWC regulations and species pages remain the authority for legal classification, limits, and current access rules.
